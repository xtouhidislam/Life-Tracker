ZERO TO VISIBLE MUSCLE

6-Month No-Equipment Calisthenics Plan

*Starting point: zero push-ups · Built for your 40-min weekday \+ longer weekend slots*

# The Honest Starting Point

Not being able to do a single push-up isn't a bad starting point — it's just information: your body needs to build the base strength before it can do the "real" version of these moves. Every exercise below has a regression (an easier variation) so you start where your body actually is, not where you wish it was. Nobody starts at a full push-up; the plan is built assuming zero base strength.

One honest truth about "visible muscle": it comes from two things together — muscle growth (this plan) and low enough body fat to see it (mostly diet). You can get strong and build real muscle on this plan regardless, but if visibility specifically matters to you, the nutrition note near the end matters as much as the workouts.

# Movement Progressions

Each pattern has a ladder. Rule: move to the next level only once you can do 3 sets of 10–12 clean reps at your current level. Don't rush the ladder — clean reps at an easier level build more real strength than sloppy reps at a harder one.

Push Progression (Chest / Shoulders / Triceps)

| Level | Exercise | Graduate When |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 1 | Wall Push-up | 3×15 feels easy |
| 2 | Incline Push-up (hands on a table/sofa edge) | 3×12 clean reps |
| 3 | Knee Push-up | 3×12 clean reps |
| 4 | Full Push-up | 3×10 clean reps |
| 5 | Diamond Push-up \+ Pike Push-up (shoulders) | 3×8–10 each |
| 6 | Decline Push-up (feet elevated) | Stretch goal for Month 5–6 |

Pull Progression (Back / Biceps)

This is the hardest pattern to train with zero equipment, since there's nothing to pull against. Two real no-equipment options, plus an optional upgrade:

* Table/Doorframe Rows — lie under a sturdy table (or hold a towel looped around a door handle) and pull your chest toward it. This is your main pulling exercise for Months 1–3.

* Superman Holds \+ Reverse Snow Angels — floor-based back activation, no equipment needed, great for posture and rear-delt/upper-back strength.

* Optional upgrade: a doorframe pull-up bar costs very little and opens up dead hangs, negative pull-ups, and eventually real pull-ups — genuinely the single best low-cost addition if you want faster back/bicep development. Entirely optional — the plan works without it.

| Level | Exercise | Graduate When |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 1 | Table/Doorframe Rows (steep angle, easier) | 3×12 clean reps |
| 2 | Table/Doorframe Rows (flatter angle, harder) | 3×12 clean reps |
| 3 | Superman Holds (20–30 sec) \+ Reverse Snow Angels | 3×15 snow angels |
| 4 (if bar available) | Dead Hang → Negative Pull-ups (slow 5-sec lower) | 3×5 clean negatives |
| 5 (if bar available) | Assisted/Jumping Pull-ups → Full Pull-up | 1 full clean rep |

# 6-Month Phase Overview

| Phase | Focus | What Changes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Month 1–2(Foundation) | Learn form, build base reps, first full push-up | Start every movement at Level 1–2. Sets: 3 per exercise. Priority is clean form and consistency, not intensity — this phase is about teaching your body the movement patterns. |
| Month 3–4(Growth) | Increase volume, progress up the ladders, add supersets | Most movements should reach Level 3–4. Sets: 3–4 per exercise, shorter rest (45–60 sec) to build muscular endurance alongside strength. This is where visible size starts building. |
| Month 5–6(Definition) | Advanced variations, higher total volume, skill moves | Push into Level 5 variations where ready (decline push-ups, pistol squats, L-sits). Add a 6th set of "finisher" circuits on Fridays/Saturdays for extra volume. This phase is about maximizing visible muscle and control. |

# Sample Session Structure (Any Day)

* Warm-up (5 min): light joint rotations, 20 jumping jacks or high knees, arm circles — never train cold.

* Main sets (25–30 min): 3–4 exercises for that day's focus, 3 sets each, at your current progression level.

* Core finisher (5 min): one core exercise from the ladder, every session regardless of the day's main focus.

* Cool-down (5 min): static stretching for the muscles trained — this is what keeps you training pain-free for 6 months, not 6 weeks.

# Visible Muscle: The Nutrition Reality

Being direct about this since it's part of your stated goal: no workout plan alone reliably produces visible muscle if nutrition isn't aligned — this is physiology, not motivation.

* Protein: aim for roughly 1.2–1.6g per kg of bodyweight per day (eggs, fish, chicken, dal/lentils, dairy — whatever fits your diet) — this is what actually builds the muscle you're training.

* If you're currently under-eating (common when very unaccustomed to training), you may need to eat slightly more, not less, in Months 1–3 to give your body the material to build muscle.

* If body fat is covering the muscle you're building, a modest calorie awareness (not a crash diet) becomes relevant more in Months 4–6, once there's actual muscle underneath worth revealing.

* This isn't a mandate to track every calorie — just be aware that training alone answers half the goal; eating enough protein and not wildly overeating answers the other half.

# Safety Principles

* Soreness for 1–2 days after a new exercise is normal; sharp pain during a movement is not — stop and regress a level if something hurts wrong, not just hard.

* Full rest on Sunday is not optional — muscle grows during recovery, not during the workout itself. Skipping rest slows progress, it doesn't speed it up.

* If a level feels too easy for all 3 sets across 2 consecutive sessions, move up — the ladder should always feel like real effort by set 3, not set 1\.

* Track your reps/sets somewhere simple (notes app, or fold this into your Sunday weekly review) — visible progress on paper is what keeps a 6-month plan from quietly fading by Month 3\.
